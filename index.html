📰 Embedded reading brief

In October 2019, Dyson announced that it would stop its electric-car project. The company said its automotive team had developed a car, but it could not see a way to make the project commercially viable. Dyson had tried to find a buyer for the project but had not succeeded.

The project involved substantial investment, specialist facilities and a large engineering team. Dyson said the decision was not a failure of the product or the team. It planned to close its automotive project facilities in the UK and Singapore, seek alternative roles for many staff, and continue developing technologies such as solid-state batteries and other advanced technologies.

Key distinction A product can demonstrate engineering achievement without having a business model that makes its production and sale commercially sustainable.

Conceptual illustration: innovation needs technical and commercial viability.

As you read, think about these questions:

  • What went well from an operations perspective?
  • What risks did Dyson face?
  • Why did the finance not work out?
  • Which part of the Innovation Triad killed the project?
